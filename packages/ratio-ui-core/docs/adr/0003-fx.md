# ADR-0003: Visual effects as a model and a renderer

## Status

Accepted 2026-11-04. Tracked in losol/ratio-ui#305.

## Context

A badge earned, a course passed, 17 May, New Year: moments an app wants to
mark with more than a toast. And a few that are not celebrations at all —
lightning for a dramatic reveal or a broken streak, snow in December. The
family has no effects today, and a library such as `canvas-confetti` gives
confetti but not fireworks or lightning, and cannot read the theme.

Effects are also where a design system can do harm. A page that flashes
more than three times a second can trigger a seizure (WCAG 2.3.1). Motion
the reader has asked to reduce, or motion during a mourning period, is
the opposite of what the moment needs.

## Decision

Two tiers, along the line ADR-0001 drew:

- **The model** is here, in `@eventuras/ratio-ui-core/fx`: particles that
  carry their own physics, one `stepParticles` for all of them, seeded
  randomness, the effects as definitions that spawn particles, their
  colours, and the rules. Pure functions in node: no canvas, no DOM.
- **The renderer** is `@eventuras/ratio-ui/fx`: one canvas over the page
  and `play()`, written without React imports, with a thin `useFx()` for
  React. `ratio-ui-native` can draw the same model later, as it draws the
  badges.

The rules live in the model, so no renderer can skip them:

- `fxMotion` turns the reader's `prefers-reduced-motion` and the page's
  `data-motion="none"` into `full`, `reduced` or `none`; `fxAllowance`
  says what each permits. Only `full` moves anything. `reduced` allows a
  still acknowledgement; `none` allows nothing.
- `admitFlash` allows at most three flashes to start in any one second,
  and `flashOpacity` holds a flash under 0.35 — dimmed, never white-out.
- Every effect has a `budget`: the most particles it may spawn, whatever
  the options say.

Effects are separate exports, so an app ships only what it uses and can
define its own `FxBurst`.

## Consequences

- The physics, the effects' shapes and the rules are unit-tested in node,
  deterministically, without a browser.
- The model's types are an interface between packages. Both are beta, and
  a change to `FxParticle` ships in both at once.
- A non-React web consumer can be served by moving `play()` into its own
  package later, without changing its API.
